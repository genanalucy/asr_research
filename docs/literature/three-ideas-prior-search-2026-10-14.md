## 1. Speaker-fold-aware adaptation allocation

**Closest direct speech-recognition work:** Jiang et al., [*Perceiver-Prompt: Flexible Speaker Adaptation in Whisper for Chinese Disordered Speech Recognition*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09873). Read: abstract, method §§2–3, experimental setup/results §4 and conclusion §5 in the five-page arXiv PDF. It first LoRA-tunes Whisper, then trains a Perceiver prompt encoder to make a fixed-length speaker prompt from variable-length utterances from that same target speaker. It also compares prompt positions/history and auxiliary speaker/FDA training. This overlaps speaker-conditioned prompts and a LoRA-plus-module recipe. Its aim is adaptation to a known dysarthric speaker using their speech, not allocating a fixed parameter budget to modules according to performance on speakers held out from the training-speaker pool. The reported improvement is task- and corpus-specific; it does not establish cross-speaker generalization for Zhuang.

Wang et al., [*Speaker Targeting via Self-Speaker Adaptation for Multi-talker ASR*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.22646) (arXiv v2, 2025). Read: abstract and method §§2–2.3 in the six-page PDF; experiment table/setup was only skimmed, so no numeric result is quoted. It injects speaker-activity-conditioned kernels into selected FastConformer encoder layers to isolate one speaker in overlapping speech. It overlaps selecting encoder locations for speaker-conditioned computation, but is a multi-talker/overlap method with frame-level speaker activity supervision, not a single-speaker low-resource PEFT allocation or speaker-held-out evaluation. It does not use Whisper.

Also found Zhang et al., [*Whisper-SV: Adapting Whisper for Low-data-resource Speaker Verification*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10048). Only the arXiv abstract was read. Its representation-selection module chooses Whisper layers with speaker-discriminative features for speaker verification. That is a close layer-selection analogy, but its output task is speaker verification (EER/minDCF), not ASR, and the abstract does not establish a fixed-parameter LoRA allocation trained against held-out-speaker ASR loss.

**Still distinct in the proposed test:** partition the verified training speakers into folds; train adapters on fold-train speakers and score the held-out training speakers; allocate a fixed LoRA parameter budget to encoder/decoder blocks using only this speaker-generalization objective; compare with uniform LoRA, dev-selected allocation, and speaker-conditioned prompt baselines. This tests population transfer across speakers rather than personalization to an enrolled/current speaker. The idea is plausible, but per-speaker folds must avoid tuning on the final dev/test speakers; current dev/test each have only two speakers, so claims about population-level variance need a larger independent speaker evaluation or careful limitation.

## 2. Audio-grounding reliability gate for teacher distillation

**Closest hallucination-detection work:** Aparin et al., [*Whisper Hallucination Detection and Mitigation via Hidden Representation Steering and Sparse AutoEncoders*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07473) (2026 preprint). Read: abstract, method §§2–3, experimental setup/results §§4.1–4.4, Tables 1–5, and conclusion in the ten-page PDF. It trains a classifier on Whisper encoder/SAE representations to detect hallucination-prone non-speech inputs and steers hidden states. It explicitly notes that Whisper’s no-speech probability plus average generated-token log probability can fail because hallucinations may have high log probability. This is a substantial nearby precedent for audio-grounding reliability and confidence-baseline comparisons. It detects non-speech/hallucination state and changes inference activations; it does not compute a teacher-forced gold-transcript score gap between matched audio and the same transcript paired with muted or deranged audio, nor use that gap as a teacher-label inclusion gate for student KD.

**Closest KD/data filtering precedent:** Gandhi et al., [*Distil-Whisper: Robust Knowledge Distillation via Large-Scale Pseudo Labelling*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.00430). Existing project note records actual read scope as abstract, §§3, 4.1–4.2, 7.1, 9.2–9.3, Tables 1/2/12 (not the full 30 pages). It combines pseudo-label sequence training with token-probability KL and filters pseudo-labels using teacher/reference WER. That is a direct precedent for teacher-error filtering and KD, but the filter is agreement with a reference transcript, not a counterfactual acoustic-grounding test.

Tripathi et al., [*Listen Like a Teacher: Mitigating Whisper Hallucinations using Adaptive Layer Attention and Knowledge Distillation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14219). Read: arXiv abstract only, located by the targeted query. Its stated setup distills a teacher processing clean audio into a student processing noisy audio, with adaptive encoder-layer attention. This is close to teacher-student robustness under audio perturbation, but not the proposed same-gold-sequence matched-vs-muted/deranged scoring rule, and the abstract alone is insufficient to establish the detailed filter or evaluation protocol.

**Still distinct in the proposed test:** for each known training pair `(audio, gold transcript)`, compute teacher-forced mean gold-token log probability under matched audio and under controlled counterfactuals (mute, time/speaker derangement, or another explicitly defined mismatch); use the score gap as an audio-grounding reliability gate or weight for KD. Compare standard teacher confidence, teacher/reference error filtering, ordinary KD, and the counterfactual gate under identical student, data, and update budget. The matched/mismatched teacher-forced score gap itself was not located in the bounded search; this is a lead to investigate, not a claim that no prior work uses contrastive audio-text scores. Keep counterfactuals semantically and technically controlled: silence can trigger special no-speech behavior, and arbitrary shuffling may create out-of-distribution inputs.

## 3. Target/donor gradient agreement for encoder block transfer

**Closest task-informed PEFT work:** Mishra et al., [*Adaptive Fisher-Whitened Cross-Covariance for Low-Resource Speech Recognition*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29800) (2026 preprint). Read: complete five-page PDF, including FCCA method, AC-FCCA cross-layer sharing, AR-FCCA adaptive rank, experimental setup/results and conclusion. It estimates Fisher-whitened input/error cross-covariance from 256 target-language calibration examples; AC-FCCA shares selected bases across adjacent layers, while AR-FCCA allocates a fixed rank budget across projection matrices according to retained task-relevant spectral energy. Thus gradient/task-statistic-guided layer sharing and capacity allocation are already a very close ASR/Whisper precedent. However, it constructs a target-task adapter subspace for Whisper-medium/Qwen3-ASR; it does not transfer encoder weights from a donor-language ASR model, keep the decoder target-only, or use target-versus-donor gradient agreement as a negative-transfer gate.

**Closest donor-selection and relatedness work:** San et al., [*Predicting Positive Transfer for Improved Low-Resource Speech Recognition Using Acoustic Pseudo-Tokens*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02302). Existing project reading card records full-text coverage: abstract, §§1–6, figures/tables, appendices A–B. It selects an XLS-R continued-pretraining donor using target/donor acoustic pseudo-token distribution similarity (ATDS), rather than donor gradient agreement or layer-wise transfer. The card reports its Punjabi and cross-language experiments and their data/compute limits. Florian et al., [*Evaluating the Effect of Linguistic Relatedness on Cross-Lingual Transfer in Large Multilingual Automatic Speech Recognition*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04814v2). Existing project note records full-text coverage §§1–6, figures/tables and limitations. It compares sequential auxiliary-language adaptation with target-only adaptation and finds genealogical relatedness alone does not yield sustained practical benefit after target adaptation in its tested settings. Neither work proposes selecting transferred encoder blocks through agreement of donor and target training gradients.

Ren et al., [*Unified Gradient Projection: Language-Balanced Continual Learning for Multilingual Low-Resource ASR*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11163) (2026 preprint). Read: arXiv abstract only. It uses reference gradients from language-balanced replay to project multilingual updates and reduce forgetting. This is a close gradient-coordination precedent, but its objective is multilingual continual learning/stability, not deciding which donor encoder blocks to copy for a new target while the decoder trains target-only.

**Still distinct in the proposed test:** initialize/copy candidate encoder blocks from a verified donor, fit the target decoder/output path using target labels only, and select or gate encoder blocks by target/donor gradient agreement measured on training data. Evaluate against target-only encoder training, full donor encoder transfer, random/uniform block transfer, and similarity-based donor choice; explicitly report negative transfer. FCCA makes “gradient-informed block allocation” alone too broad to claim as new. A narrower novelty candidate is the *cross-language donor-to-target block transfer decision rule* and the controlled encoder-only/decoder-target-only factorization, subject to further comparison against gradient-based transfer, LoRA-GA, and any source-language layer selection literature. Donor language identity/data provenance must be verified first; the current Yao corpus is not ready to serve as an authenticated donor.

## Practical reading

The three proposals are not equally clear of prior work. Idea 1 has direct speaker-conditioned adaptation and layer-selection analogies, but its held-out-speaker objective is distinct from per-speaker personalization and speaker verification. Idea 2 has very close recent hallucination detection and KD filtering work; the counterfactual teacher-forced likelihood-gap gate is the clearest unlocated mechanism in this pass, though audio-text contrastive scoring requires more search. Idea 3 overlaps strongly with 2026 task-informed FCCA and earlier acoustic donor ranking; only donor/target gradient-agreement gating of transferred encoder blocks appears distinct in this limited comparison.

Before making a novelty claim, search the same exact mechanisms in ACL Anthology, Interspeech/ISCA, IEEE Xplore, and OpenReview, and inspect citations/references of the close papers. The arXiv API and some DuckDuckGo requests were used here; some DuckDuckGo responses returned bot checks. No exhaustive database search was performed.
